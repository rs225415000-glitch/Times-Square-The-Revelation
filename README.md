56  Your text is beautifully poetic, framing Times Square as a "temple of pilgrimage" for humanity. Scientifically validating this statement requires looking at the data of human movement, urban geography, anthropology, and sociology.
Data and research strongly support the core themes of your quote: unrivaled global diversity, dense human convergence ("gravity"), and peaceful coexistence.
"Behold the gravity of thine own kind" (Human Density & Convergence)
The use of the word "gravity" is a perfect metaphor for what urban physicists call the Gravity Model of Migration. This model states that larger, more significant places exert a magnetic pull that attracts people from vast distances.
The Data: According to the Times Square Alliance, Times Square is the most visited pedestrian intersection in the world, with roughly 330,000 to 460,000 pedestrians walking through it every single day. 
The Science: This massive flow amounts to nearly 131 million people per year. Geographically, there is no other open, outdoor public space on Earth where so many individual humans from different origins cross paths purely for leisure and exploration. 
 "People of every race, nation, and creed" (Global Diversity)
Your sentence claims that you will find all "manifold differences" gathered here. Demographic and tourism statistics back this up perfectly.
The Data: Times Square's crowd shifts based on the season, but overall data shows an incredibly high baseline of diversity. Roughly 50% of the people present are NYC locals (who represent one of the most ethnically diverse cities on Earth), 33% are domestic US travelers, and 17% to 38% are international tourists. 
The Reach: During peak summer and holiday seasons, the international presence spikes to 47% to 52% of the entire crowd. According to NYC Tourism reports, these millions of international travelers represent virtually every recognized nation, led heavily by the UK, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, France, Italy, and China. 
 "Gathered together in peace" (Sociological Peace)
From a sociological standpoint, packing half a million people from entirely different religious, political, and cultural backgrounds into a tight 7-block radius should be a recipe for conflict. Instead, it functions as a peaceful hub.
The Science: Sociologists call this "Civic Inattention" and "Cosmopolitan Canopy." Coined by sociologist Elijah Anderson, a cosmopolitan canopy is a safe urban space where people of different backgrounds practice mutual respect and coexistence.
The Data: Despite the extreme density, violent crime rates within the pedestrian plazas of Times Square are incredibly low relative to the volume of people. Humans in Times Square naturally self-regulate their movement—subconsciously utilizing fluid dynamics to weave past one another without collision or conflict.
"That which bindeth them is greater" (Shared Human Psychology)
Why do they gather peacefully? Because they are bound by a shared psychological state.
The Science: Environmental psychology shows that Times Square functions as an "Awe-Inspirer." The massive, hyper-bright LED billboards and architectural scale trigger a psychological state of awe.
The Impact: Research in Psychological Science notes that experiencing awe minimizes the "self" and makes individuals feel part of a collective. When people feel smaller in a grand environment, they become more prosocial, generous, and empathetic to strangers around them. They are bound together by the shared, neutral identity of being a "visitor" or "explorer."
